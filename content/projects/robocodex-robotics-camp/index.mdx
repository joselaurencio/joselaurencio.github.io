## Problem

Younger students interested in robotics in the San Antonio area had very few
structured, hands-on entry points before high school FTC/FLL teams — most
exposure was either a single-day demo or nothing at all.

## Constraints

- Volunteer-run, no dedicated facility — everything had to work inside a
  church classroom space with folding tables.
- A single week to take students from "never touched a robot" to building and
  programming something that moves and does a task.
- Budget was sponsor-funded, not fee-funded — materials had to be reusable
  across cohorts.

## Design Process

Split the week into a beginner track (block-based programming, guided kit
builds) and an advanced track (more open-ended build challenges, intro to
text-based code), running in parallel with shared "showcase" time at the end
of each day so both tracks saw what the other built.

## Iterations & Failures

The first draft of the curriculum tried to cover too much ground in too
little time — sessions ran long and kids lost interest by day three. The
schedule was rebuilt around shorter, more frequent build-test-iterate loops
instead of long lecture blocks.

## Testing & Validation

Piloted the curriculum with a small first cohort before opening it more
broadly, using end-of-day feedback from both students and parent volunteers
to catch pacing problems early.

## Optimization

Reworked material kits so each station could be reset for the next group in
under five minutes, since the shared-space constraint meant no dedicated
storage between sessions.

## Final Result

Ran a full week-long camp for a first cohort of students across both tracks,
with a structured curriculum, camp materials, and visual branding built for
reuse in future cohorts.

## Lessons Learned

Camp design is a different engineering problem than robot design — the
constraint that mattered most wasn't the robotics kit, it was attention span
and group dynamics.

## Future Improvements

The next cohort adds a track for returning students and starts publishing
the curriculum so it isn't locked to one camp.
