## Problem

Build a robot for FIRST Tech Challenge competition capable of scoring
consistently under a strict match clock, driven and iterated on by a young
team in its first few seasons of competition.

## Constraints

- Season-length build window, shared with school and camp commitments.
- Standard FTC hardware/software rules (control system, game-specific
  scoring elements, field dimensions) set by FIRST each season.
- Small team — design, build, and code all had to be owned by the same few
  people across every subsystem.

## Design Process

Subsystems were built and tested independently (drivetrain, scoring
mechanism, autonomous routine) before integration, so a failure in one
subsystem didn't block testing on the others.

## Iterations & Failures

Early scoring-mechanism prototypes worked in isolation but jammed once
mounted on the full chassis due to wiring routing that hadn't accounted for
the mechanism's full range of motion — resolved by rerouting and adding a
cable-management pass as a standing step in every build iteration afterward.

## Testing & Validation

Ran full match simulations against practice-field mockups ahead of each
event, specifically testing the autonomous routine against the actual field
tile seams that caused drift issues that don't show up on smooth flooring.

## Optimization

Tuned autonomous routine timing and drivetrain PID values iteratively between
events based on match footage review, not just practice-field results.

## Final Result

Season results across events: Dean's List Semifinalist, Inspire Award (1st),
and Finalist Award (2nd) at the Adventist Robotics Championship — the
strongest season result on record for the team. Full record on the
[Robotics hub](/robotics).

## Lessons Learned

The judged interview mattered as much as the field performance — being able
to explain *why* a design decision was made, including the failed attempts,
consistently mattered more than a polished final answer with no visible
process behind it.

## Future Improvements

Next season's build starts from a more general autonomous approach and a
running practice log, both aimed at cutting the rebuild-from-memory time
before each event.
